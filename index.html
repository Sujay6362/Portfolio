Research

Dynamic FPCA-Based Cognitive Trajectory Prediction in Alzheimer's Disease

This research focuses on predicting longitudinal cognitive decline in Alzheimer's disease using the National Alzheimer's Coordinating Center (NACC) dataset. The study utilized 18,516 patients with at least three valid visits, resulting in over 125,000 longitudinal records spanning up to 19 years. Instead of predicting single cognitive scores, the objective was to model the entire trajectory of decline using real-time clinical progression data.

To achieve this, Functional Principal Component Analysis (FPCA) was applied to compress each patient's full NACCMMSE trajectory into two interpretable components: one capturing the rate and shape of decline, and the other representing baseline cognitive level. A key methodological innovation was constructing trajectories using actual calendar time (days since first visit) rather than visit indices, ensuring biologically meaningful temporal modeling. Age-stratified FPCA further improved representation by capturing population-specific progression patterns.

A hybrid feature architecture was designed by separating 104 selected variables into static baseline features (e.g., genetics, demographics, family history) and dynamic longitudinal features (e.g., cognitive tests, CDR scores, vitals). A cumulative learning framework was implemented using Random Forest models, where each successive year incorporates all prior patient history along with newly observed clinical data, mimicking real-world clinical decision-making.

The model demonstrated strong predictive performance, with trajectory prediction (R²) improving from 0.35 at baseline to 0.91 by Year 5. Baseline cognitive level prediction improved from 0.30 to 0.82 over the same period. Key predictors included visuospatial ability (PENTAGON), family history variables, and vascular health indicators. The results highlight that longitudinal data accumulation significantly enhances predictive accuracy and that early trajectory prediction becomes reliable after approximately two years of observation.

This work introduces a scalable framework for modeling irregular longitudinal clinical data using FPCA and cumulative machine learning, providing a foundation for early intervention strategies and personalized disease monitoring in neurodegenerative disorders.
